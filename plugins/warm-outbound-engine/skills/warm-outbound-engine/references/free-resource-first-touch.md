# The free resource first touch

Build each qualified lead a small, genuinely useful asset from their own company's data, offer it in the first message, and hand it over when they reply. The message stops being a pitch and becomes a delivery. This is the part of agnt_'s own LinkedIn outbound that the sourcing recipe alone does not cover: in that system a second agent read each qualified lead's website, worked out what AI agent would actually help their operation, pre-built it, and the outreach offered it with a claim link. It was described end to end in "Run Your Entire Business on AI Agents" (YouTube `s3iAOMQm7gE`, from 1:21:38). The asset there was an agent because agents are what agnt_ sells. The principle generalizes to anything the user's business is already good at making.

## TL;DR

- Offer a thing, not a meeting. "Hey, I built you an agent for <company>, it does X" replaced "quick question about your outbound" (s3iAOMQm7gE @ 1:24:56).
- Two effects stack. The message doesn't read as a hard sell because you're handing them something, and because the asset is built off their actual company nobody mistakes it for a template (@ 1:26:24).
- Offer in the first touch, deliver on the reply. In the running system the first message described the asset, and the reply agent sent the link to the leads who answered (@ 1:25:49). The reply is the conversion event.
- Reply speed matters as much as the asset. The same agent answered interested replies quickly because "it's really important to reply quickly to leads that are replying to you" (@ 1:25:30). Pair this with `webhook-auto-reply.md`.
- Build only for leads that already cleared the ICP score. An asset costs more than any enrichment call.
- The asset must be real, specific and correct. A wrong fact about someone's business costs more than sending nothing.
- Observed: the HeyReach campaign running this motion showed a 55.3% reply rate over a 7-day window (@ 1:22:14). One account, one week, and a product where the asset is the product demo. Treat it as the ceiling to test against, not a promise, and never quote it to a user as their expected result.
- Prove the lift before scaling it: split qualified leads between an asset opener and a plain opener and compare reply rates.

## Why it works

Alex Hormozi's lead-magnet doctrine is the theory under this play, and it survives the move from gyms and agencies to B2B outbound almost intact.

- **Value first creates the prediction people buy on.** People buy on a prediction of future value, and the easiest way to create that prediction is to deliver value before they buy (Hormozi `7NMH1oAkgLY` @ 00:22).
- **The offer beats the copy.** He puts the lead magnet straight into the cold message and calls it the biggest response-rate lever there is, bigger than the wording (`Jg5ziFeG6-k` @ 08:00, 21:16). In the same interview the host reported a 4:1 response rate for a free-audit ask over a book-a-call ask (`Jg5ziFeG6-k` @ 13:01; the host's number, not Hormozi's).
- **For strangers, give a slice of the service, not information.** Info lead magnets are "overdone" and presumed to be fluff; free work that visibly cost something is valued far higher (`Jg5ziFeG6-k` @ 09:09; `7NMH1oAkgLY` @ 06:45). An AI-built asset is information-shaped, so it only escapes that presumption when it is unmistakably about their company, correct, and something they can act on.
- **Solve one narrow problem completely, and let it reveal the next.** Never a teaser, which loses trust; the solved problem should expose the next one, which the paid product fixes. Don't give away the core job itself (`7NMH1oAkgLY` @ 01:14, 01:24; `Mst4hreQYl0` @ 02:01, 09:52).
- **Take it from your own onboarding.** Of the many small things done for every new customer, pick the one that is high value, fast and repeatable, and do it up front for the prospect (`Jg5ziFeG6-k` @ 09:50, 10:04).
- **Anchor the value to the market.** Give something other people charge for, and say so only when it's true (`Jg5ziFeG6-k` @ 08:36, 08:50).
- **The quality bar goes up with AI, not down.** "Your free stuff should be better than their paid stuff," and a lead magnet that isn't good enough for people to pass along counts as a failure (`7NMH1oAkgLY` @ 07:09, 10:55). AI pushes the cost of making each one toward zero; a templated-looking asset pushes its perceived value there too.
- **Name it by the result, not the vehicle.** Changing only the name of a lead magnet can move demand several times over; "a free case study" filled a calendar that "a webinar" could not (`Mst4hreQYl0` @ 04:29, 05:12, 23:37).

Nick Abraham, who runs this exact engager motion at agency scale, lands in the same place from the practitioner side:

- **Service lead magnets win, by a distance.** "Service lead magnets, by far, are getting the best results": a free deliverable tied directly to the paid outcome, like a lead-gen agency offering to build a SaaS-focused list for a company that sells to SaaS (Nick Abraham `ofv0vRF1p9I` @ 02:49, 03:12).
- **The offer beats the personalization.** "0 personalization + 10/10 offer beats 10/10 personalization + 6/10 offer" (Nick Abraham on X, 2026-03-04).
- **The test is "why would they say no to this?"** A good one is fast, repeatable enough to template, at least partly automatable, and high in perceived value. Generic audits and free consultations fail it, because everyone knows they end in a pitch (`ofv0vRF1p9I` @ 03:58, 04:16, 05:08).
- **His engager opener is built on it:** "Saw you're engaging with a ton of {service} content on LinkedIn. Would love to {give free value}. Open to it?", because the prospect has shown intent, is offered value, and the message reads as personal (X, 2024-04-22).

## What counts as a good asset

Run every asset idea through these six checks. It needs all six.

1. **Built from their data.** Their website, their LinkedIn profile, their company data, the post they engaged with and, for commenters, what they said. If the same asset could go to a different company unchanged, it's a template and it will read like one.
2. **Useful in under two minutes.** A short page, a working thing, or three to five findings. A 30-page PDF is homework.
3. **About one narrow problem they have, solved completely.** Ideally the problem the post they engaged with was about. That's the one thing you know they were thinking about this week.
4. **A slice of what the user sells.** The asset is one step of the paid work, done once for free, and finishing it should make the next step obvious. A reader who finds it useful should immediately see what the full version would be.
5. **Cheap at volume.** Minutes of agent time and cents of data per lead. If it needs a human to make each one, it's a different motion.
6. **True.** Every number has a source, and exact figures beat ranges. No invented metrics, no claims about their internals the agent can't see, no "I noticed your churn is up" guesses dressed as findings.

## The asset menu

Hormozi's three shapes cover almost everything: **reveal a problem** (an assessment that shows what's wrong and how much it costs, his favorite), **a sample** of the product or service, or **one step** of a multi-step process done for them (`Mst4hreQYl0` @ 06:00, 06:31, 10:24). Ask the user once which of these fits (step 1 stores the answer as `icp_profile.first_touch_asset`). If none fits, work one out with them from check 4: what would a slice of their paid work look like?

| What the user sells | The asset | Built from |
|---|---|---|
| AI agents, automation, software | A working setup of their product for the lead's company (a pre-configured workspace, workflow or agent) with a claim or invite link | Their site and role; the user's product must support a pre-built trial or share link |
| Outbound, lead gen, recruiting, data | A sample list: 10 to 25 people or accounts that match the lead's own ICP, each with why they fit | This skill's own steps 2 to 4 run on the lead's market, or `data_agnt_people_search` / `data_agnt_companies_search` |
| SEO, content, growth | A gap report: 3 to 5 search queries their competitors win and they don't, with the page each needs | SEO data tools if the workspace has them, otherwise `data_web_scrape` of their site and two competitors |
| Web design, CRO, dev shops | A teardown of their homepage or pricing page: three fixes with the reasoning for each | `data_web_scrape` of the page |
| Consulting, ops, finance, agencies | A one-page plan for the problem they commented about | Their comment, their site, their company data |
| Analytics, research, strategy | A benchmark: how they compare with three named competitors on the one metric their role cares about | `data_agnt_companies_enrich` + `data_web_scrape` |

The sample-list row is the natural fit for anyone selling outbound: run a small version of this skill for the lead ("I found 12 people who engaged with posts about <their problem> this week and look like your buyers. Want the list?"). It proves the product with the product.

## The flow

This runs inside step 5 of the skill, between the score and staging.

1. **Only qualified leads.** `status = 'enriched'` rows above the score bar, highest `fit_score` first, capped per run (default 10; the user sets it). Commenters before reactors, because they said something you can build on.
2. **Research the lead.** `data_linkedin_get_profile_data_by_url`, their recent posts with `data_linkedin_get_profile_s_posts`, the company with `data_agnt_companies_enrich`, their site with `data_web_scrape` (`agnt_tools_enable({ "families": ["data:web"] })` if it is missing), plus the source post and their `engagement_text`. Stop at what the asset needs; this is the expensive part of the run.
3. **Build it.** Write it to a `lead_assets` row: `lead_id`, `kind`, `title`, `summary` (one line the opener can quote), `body` or `url`, `sources`, `status` (draft, approved, sent, opened, claimed), `built_at`.
4. **Host it where the user already shares things**: their product, a doc in their workspace, a page on their site. The platform option for data-shaped assets (lists, benchmarks, scorecards) is a canvas whose widgets read that lead's `lead_assets` rows, shared with `agnt_canvas_share_create`. A share link opens for anyone without an account, so get the user's go-ahead for public asset links once, and `agnt_canvas_share_revoke` the link if the lead asks to be left alone.
5. **Quality gate.** The user reviews the first five assets before any message references them. After that, show one in ten. A bad asset at volume is the fastest way to burn a warm list.
6. **Write the opener around it** (templates below) and stage it in the chosen lane like every other message. Nothing changes about the stage-never-start rule.
7. **Deliver on reply.** When the lead answers, send the link in the thread, by hand or through the auto-reply agent in `webhook-auto-reply.md`. Set `lead_assets.status = 'sent'`.
8. **Measure.** Tag every staged lead `opener_variant = 'asset'` or `'plain'` and report reply rate per variant per lane. Keep a plain arm running until the asset arm has won on at least 100 sends.

## Copy

Adapt these to the playbook's length and tone rules (`cold-outreach-playbook.md`). Name the specific thing; never say "a free resource". Two rules from the research apply to every template:

- **Topic, never the creator.** Say "saw you've been in a lot of conversations about <topic>", never "saw your comment on <creator>'s post". Name-drops read as surveillance and get screenshotted back to the creator (Nick Abraham `gbdzLWhdmVM` @ 06:01; X, 2026-03-12).
- **Keep the word "free" out of cold email.** Filters fingerprint it: removing it took one campaign from under 1% to over 3% reply (Nick Abraham on X, 2026-05-18). "Yours either way" does the same job.

**LinkedIn connection request.** Blank, as the playbook says. The asset goes in the first message after they accept, never in the request.

**First LinkedIn message after the accept:**
> Saw you've been in a lot of conversations about <topic> lately. I put together <asset> for <company>: <one specific finding or what it does>. Want me to send it over?

**First email (Instantly, AgentMail or Gmail).** Lowercase subject of two to five words, such as `built this for <company>`. No link in the first email: links hurt cold deliverability, and the reply is the point.
> <first name>, I built <company> a <asset> after going through <their site / their pricing page / their last few posts>. <one concrete finding>. It's yours either way, want me to send it?

A longer shape for email, from a cold email Hormozi called fundamentally sound (`Jg5ziFeG6-k` @ 24:53 to 25:45): a first line citing one specific, real detail about them; a question about the problem; one line on why you ask; a permission ask ("could I send you <asset>?"); and a PS saying part of it is already done for them. His only edit to that email was to replace a range with the exact number.

**Follow-up when there's no reply (3 to 4 days later).** Offer a different angle rather than bumping the same one:
> If <asset> isn't the useful one, I can do <alternative A> or <alternative B> instead. Which is closer?

**The delivery, when they reply:**
> Here it is: <link>. Start with <the one part worth looking at first>. If you want it adapted to <their situation>, tell me what to change.

No pitch in the delivery. The value lands first; the ask comes in the next exchange, once they've engaged with it.

**Optional P.S. for users who sell agents or automation**, and only when it's literally true: "The agent that built this also wrote this message." It's the product demo in one line.

## Guardrails

- **Never fabricate.** No invented numbers, no pretending the agent analyzed data it guessed at. Put the sources on the asset.
- **Public information only.** Their website, public profile, public posts. Never logged-in, private or scraped-behind-auth data.
- **Qualified leads only.** A disqualified lead never gets an asset, however cheap it would be.
- **Budget cap per lead.** Stop building when the run's cap is hit and report what's left.
- **Stage, never start.** An asset doesn't change the skill's hard rules. Links leave only through a staged sequence the user starts or a reply the user has approved (or pre-approved as a template).
- **Honor a no.** If a lead says no or asks to stop, revoke the share link, mark the row and never message them again.
- **Kill it if it doesn't win.** If the asset arm doesn't beat the plain arm after about 100 sends each, stop building assets and put the effort back into targeting.

## Contested and context-dependent

- **The 55.3% figure** comes from one account over 7 days, selling agents, where the asset was the product itself. Businesses whose asset sits further from what they sell will see less. The skill page's own proof (36% reply over 8 weeks) is the sourcing recipe without assets.
- **Link in the first message or on reply.** The long-form post version says "here's the link, it's yours"; the video version delivers on reply. Default to deliver-on-reply for both LinkedIn and email. A link in the first LinkedIn DM after an accept is a reasonable variant to test, never in a cold email.
- **Cost.** An asset can cost ten times the enrichment for the same lead. The per-run cap and the qualified-only rule are what keep it a sourcing play rather than an agency service.
